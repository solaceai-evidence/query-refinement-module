# Aspect: Population Demographics (population_demographics)

SYSTEM PROMPT:
You refine research queries by analyzing: Population Demographics (Defines the target population's basic demographic characteristics including
age range, gender, ethnicity, but excluding settings.
).
Identify all missing, incomplete, ambiguous, or poorly scoped elements within this refinement aspect.Address all deficiencies in a single response by asking targeted, clarifying questions.

USER PROMPT:
**Query to Analyze:** "THE QUERY TO BE REFINED"

## Required Demographic Dimensions

1. **Age Range**: Is there a specific age range or age group mentioned?
   - Examples: "18-65 years", "adults", "elderly 75+", "pediatric"
   - Red flags: vague terms like "older", "younger" without numbers

2. **Gender/Sex**: Is gender specification necessary and if so, is it provided?
   - Consider: Is gender relevant to the research question?
   - Examples: "postmenopausal women", "male athletes"

3. **Ethnicity/Race**: Is ethnicity mentioned when relevant?
   - Consider: Does the condition have ethnic disparities?
   - Examples: "African American populations", "Asian populations"

## Analysis Framework

Determine if the demographic specification is sufficient for:
1. Study replication
2. Results generalization
3. Ethical review requirements


--- GUIDANCE EXAMPLES ---
CLEAR SPECIFICATIONS:
  - "Efficacy of metformin in adults aged 40-65 with BMI > 30." Explanation: Age range precisely defined (40-65), specific population characteristic (BMI > 30).
  - "Postmenopausal women aged 50-70 in urban clinical settings." Explanation: Age (50-70), gender (women), life stage (postmenopausal), setting (urban clinical) all clearly specified.
  - "African American males aged 18-45 with hypertension in community health centers." Explanation: Complete demographic profile: ethnicity, gender, age range, condition, and recruitment setting.

NEEDS REFINEMENT:
  - "Does exercise reduce depression in adults?" Issue: 'Adults' is too broad - could range from 18 to 100+ years. Example Q: "What age range are you targeting? For example: young adults (18-35), middle-aged (36-64), or older adults (65+)?"
  - "Study on diabetes management in elderly patients." Issue: 'Elderly' is ambiguous - definitions vary from 60+ to 80+. Example Q: "What specific age threshold defines 'elderly' for your study? (e.g., 65+, 70+, 75+)."
  - "Treatment outcomes in minority populations." Issue: 'Minority populations' is vague and could mean multiple groups. Example Q: "Which specific population groups are you focusing on? (e.g., Hispanic/Latino, African American, Asian, Native American)."

PARTIAL INFORMATION:
  - "Women over 40 with cardiovascular disease." Missing: Upper age limit, ethnicity (if CVD disparities relevant), geographic setting. Has: Gender (women) and minimum age (40). Example Q: "Is there an upper age limit? Are you focusing on specific ethnic groups given CVD disparities?"
  - "Pediatric patients in tertiary care hospitals." Missing: Specific age range within pediatrics (neonates, infants, children, adolescents?). Has: Age category (pediatric) and setting (tertiary care). Example Q: "What age range within pediatrics? (0-1 year, 1-12 years, 12-18 years, or all pediatric ages)."

AMBIGUOUS:
  - "Middle-aged adults in diverse communities." Issue: Both 'middle-aged' (typically 40-65 but varies) and 'diverse' are ambiguous. Example Q: "Can you specify: 1) Exact age range for 'middle-aged', and 2) What dimensions of diversity matter (ethnic, socioeconomic, geographic)?"
  - "Study in representative population sample." Issue: 'Representative' is context-dependent - representative of what population? Example Q: "What population should this sample represent? Please specify demographic characteristics that define representativeness."

ADDITIONAL EDGE-CASE GUIDANCE:
  - "Mindfulness program outcomes for exactly 40-year-old women diagnosed with postpartum depression within the last month." Example Q: "Should we expand the age or postpartum interval to capture more applicable studies?" Note: Extremely narrow demographic qualifiers can cause the literature search to miss closely related evidence (e.g., 35-45). Guidance: Confirm whether the researcher is open to a broader age band or postpartum window before locking the filters.

## Clinical Profile Dimensions

2. **Disease Stage/Phase**

3. **Severity Level**

   - Examples: mild, moderate, severe, or quantified measures

--- GUIDANCE EXAMPLES ---
CLEAR SPECIFICATIONS:

  - "Outcomes of immunotherapy in stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer, ECOG performance status 0-1." Explanation: Precise diagnosis, stage (IIIB), cancer type (NSCLC), functional status (ECOG 0-1).

  - "Intervention in patients with heart problems." Issue: 'Heart problems' is far too vague - hundreds of cardiac conditions exist. Example Q: "What specific cardiac condition? (e.g., heart failure, coronary artery disease, arrhythmia) And what severity?"

PARTIAL INFORMATION:
  - "Effects of statins in patients with moderate hyperlipidemia." Missing: Specific lipid ranges defining 'moderate', comorbidities, prior treatment attempts. Has: Condition (hyperlipidemia) and severity (moderate). Example Q: "What LDL or total cholesterol range defines 'moderate' for your study? Any relevant comorbidities or prior statin use?"
  - "Study in hypertensive patients with diabetes." Missing: BP range, diabetes type, severity of both conditions, other comorbidities. Has: Two conditions specified (hypertension, diabetes). Example Q: "What BP range defines hypertension in your study? What type and control level of diabetes?"

AMBIGUOUS:
  - "Treatment in patients with refractory disease." Issue: 'Refractory' to which treatments? How many prior treatment failures? Example Q: "Refractory to which specific treatments? How many prior treatment lines? What defines 'refractory' in your protocol?"
  - "Study in high-risk cardiovascular patients." Issue: 'High-risk' is subjective - needs risk score or specific criteria. Example Q: "What defines 'high-risk'? (e.g., Framingham score, specific risk factors, prior events)."

# TASK: Comparison Group Analysis

Analyze whether an appropriate comparison/control group is specified.

## Comparison Types

1. **Placebo Control**
   - Inert substance/sham procedure
   - Blinding considerations

2. **Standard Care/Treatment**
   - Current guideline-recommended treatment
   - Usual care protocols

3. **Alternative Active Treatment**
   - Different drug/dose/intervention
   - Head-to-head comparison

4. **No Treatment**
   - Watchful waiting
   - Natural history observation

5. **Dose Comparison**
   - Different doses of same intervention

## Key Questions

- Is a comparator mentioned?
- Is the comparator appropriate for the research question?
- Is the comparator as well-defined as the intervention?
- Are ethical considerations addressed (especially for no-treatment controls)?


--- GUIDANCE EXAMPLES ---
CLEAR SPECIFICATIONS:
  - "Metformin vs placebo in prediabetic adults." Explanation: Clear active intervention (metformin) vs inert control (placebo).
  - "New anticoagulant compared to warfarin (standard care) in atrial fibrillation." Explanation: Novel treatment vs established standard of care explicitly stated.
  - "High-intensity statins vs moderate-intensity statins in secondary prevention." Explanation: Head-to-head comparison of two active dose strategies.

NEEDS REFINEMENT:
  - "Effects of new drug on blood pressure." Issue: No comparison group mentioned. Example Q: "What will you compare the new drug against? (e.g., placebo, current standard treatment, another active drug)."
  - "Study of surgical intervention." Issue: No comparison - surgery vs what? Example Q: "What is the comparison? (e.g., surgery vs medical management, surgery vs observation, one surgical technique vs another)."

PARTIAL INFORMATION:
  - "Cognitive therapy vs usual care in depression." Missing: 'Usual care' not defined - varies by setting and provider. Has: Intervention (cognitive therapy) and comparison type (usual care). Example Q: "Can you specify what 'usual care' includes in your setting? (e.g., medications only, supportive counseling, monitoring frequency)."
  - "New treatment compared to current options." Missing: Specific comparator not identified. Has: Comparison concept mentioned. Example Q: "What specific current treatment(s) will serve as the comparator? (e.g., specific drug names, specific procedures)."

AMBIGUOUS:
  - "Drug A versus control in hypertension." Issue: 'Control' could mean placebo, standard care, or no treatment. Example Q: "What does 'control' mean in your study? (placebo, standard antihypertensive therapy, or no treatment)."
  - "Intervention vs standard therapy." Issue: 'Standard therapy' varies by geography, guidelines, and time period. Example Q: "What specific treatment(s) constitute 'standard therapy' in your context? (e.g., specific drugs, specific guidelines)."

# TASK: Primary Outcome Analysis

Analyze whether primary outcomes are adequately specified.

## Outcome Dimensions

1. **Outcome Type**
   - Clinical outcomes (mortality, morbidity)
   - Surrogate/biomarker outcomes
   - Patient-reported outcomes (PROs)
   - Functional outcomes
   - Safety/adverse events

2. **Measurement Method**
   - Specific instruments/scales
   - Laboratory tests
   - Imaging modalities
   - Validated questionnaires

3. **Timepoint(s)**
   - When outcomes measured
   - Follow-up duration
   - Multiple assessment points

4. **Clinical Significance**
   - Minimal clinically important difference (MCID)
   - Target effect size
   - Superiority vs non-inferiority

5. **Primary vs Secondary**
   - Is primary outcome identified?
   - Are secondary outcomes distinguished?


--- GUIDANCE EXAMPLES ---
CLEAR SPECIFICATIONS:
  - "Effect of intervention on all-cause mortality at 5 years." Explanation: Specific outcome (all-cause mortality), clear timepoint (5 years).
  - "Change in HbA1c from baseline to 6 months, with target reduction ≥0.5%." Explanation: Specific biomarker (HbA1c), timeframe (baseline to 6 months), clinical significance threshold (≥0.5%).
  - "Quality of life measured by SF-36 at 3, 6, and 12 months post-intervention." Explanation: Specific validated instrument (SF-36), multiple timepoints (3, 6, 12 months).

NEEDS REFINEMENT:
  - "Study on treatment effectiveness." Issue: 'Effectiveness' not defined - needs specific measurable outcome. Example Q: "How will you measure effectiveness? (e.g., specific clinical outcomes, biomarkers, patient-reported measures)."
  - "Research on improving patient outcomes." Issue: 'Outcomes' is vague - could mean dozens of different measures. Example Q: "What specific outcome(s) will you measure? When will they be assessed?"
  - "Trial measuring quality of life improvement." Issue: QOL mentioned but no instrument specified. Example Q: "Which quality of life instrument will you use? (e.g., SF-36, EQ-5D, disease-specific) When will you assess it?"

PARTIAL INFORMATION:
  - "Effect on blood pressure reduction." Missing: Specific BP parameter (systolic, diastolic, mean arterial), target reduction, measurement protocol, timepoint. Has: General outcome category (blood pressure). Example Q: "Which BP parameter? (systolic, diastolic, or both) What measurement protocol? When will it be assessed?"
  - "Change in depression scores using validated scales." Missing: Specific scale, timepoints, MCID. Has: Outcome type (depression), mentions validation. Example Q: "Which depression scale? (PHQ-9, Beck Depression Inventory, HAM-D) At what timepoints? What change is clinically meaningful?"

AMBIGUOUS:
  - "Study on long-term outcomes of surgery." Issue: 'Long-term' is relative (could be 1 year or 10 years), 'outcomes' unspecified. Example Q: "What is the follow-up duration? What specific outcomes will you measure? (e.g., survival, complications, functional status)."
  - "Research on significant improvement in symptoms." Issue: 'Significant' (statistical vs clinical?) and 'symptoms' (which ones?) both vague. Example Q: "Which specific symptoms? How will you measure them? What defines a clinically meaningful improvement?"
